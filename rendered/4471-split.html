Counts are built from reports-4471.json at render time, one report per id: the export pages overlap, so some reports come twice. A report belongs to a problem by its tenant and how it came in (causes.ts, split.ts), not by intake's tag. Intake guesses from the reporter's browser, and a browser set to en-US says nothing about the tenant's date order.

1 · The parser reads day-first dates month first

Every tenant in #4471 writes the day first. The shared parser reads month first, so 7 April becomes 4 July. Any date with a day of 12 or less flips; a day above 12 cannot be a month, so those dates survive. This is the fix already queued.

2 · booking-v3 tells day-first tenants to type month first

booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. On a day-first tenant (alder.physio, copper.studio, pale.harbour) people type what the hint says, and the form reads it the tenant's way. The result looks the same as problem 1: day and month swapped. The parser fix does not touch it. The fix is the hint, in the form: print the tenant's own order. booking-v2 prints DD/MM/YYYY and is not affected. Only reports sent through the booking-v3 form belong here; the same tenants' app and email reports are problem 1.

3 · Dates move by one day

These reports are not a swap. The invite and the calendar disagree by exactly one day: 12 May shows as 11 May, 6 October as Monday 5 October, one attendee in Lisbon gets Tuesday and one in Toronto gets Monday, an invite arrives before the booking was made, every booking in June is a day off. That is a time zone or daylight-saving shift, not a date order. The desk noted these reports itself (desk-notes.json, oneDayMove), then filed them under problem 1 by tenant. Neither fix moves them back.

How we got one ticket

Decision 0001 merges on subject line, and every one of these reports says some version of "wrong date". Same subject is not same cause. The triage explorer shows it: same subject, different tenant gives different causes.

Lessons

  • A ticket count is not a bug count. Split by cause before you mark a big ticket known.
  • Read the desk's own notes: the third problem was already written down.
